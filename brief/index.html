Five years of Dubai Land Department sales. The value of property sold went from AED 71.7B in 2020 to AED 680B in 2025, about ten times. 2020 was the pandemic year, so it is a low starting point, but every year since has grown. The 2021 and 2022 figures are not in the DLD reports we have, so we estimated them from DLD transaction data, adding up every sales registration; that method matches DLD's reported totals for 2023, 2024 and 2025 within 0.5%. Off-plan, where the developer pays the commission, was 63% of sales in 2025. 2026 is AED 423.4B to 10 November. The dashed box shows the AED 257B still needed to match 2025; it is a gap, not a forecast, as the market slowed in the second half.

Ras Al Khaimah is the third market. Completed property sales rose from AED 1.0B in 2020 to AED 3.2B in 2025, more than three times. 2024 peaked at AED 6.4B, lifted by AED 2.65B of tourism land sold in ten deals. 2026 to September is AED 2.1B, already 67% of the 2025 total in nine months; the dashed box shows the AED 1.1B still needed to match 2025. The average sale almost doubled, from AED 0.56M in 2020 to AED 1.08M in 2025. These are completed sales only: RAK reports off-plan sales separately, AED 11.2B in 2025.

02The problem · Developers

Developers carry commission costs on every sale

Paying brokers on time strains cash flow.

Developerpays commission toBrokeragepays a share toBroker

The need

A partner that pays brokers now and is repaid later

Most exposed

Smaller developers

Limited access to funding, so commission is paid from project cash.

The commission starts with the developer. Every sale creates a commission bill, and paying every broker on time strains cash flow. Developers would like a partner who pays the brokers now and lets the developer pay later. Smaller developers have it worst, because they can't raise funds the way large developers can.

02The problem · Brokerages

Brokerages cannot forecast commission income

Earlier payment would fund the marketing that drives developer sales.

Next in line is the brokerage. It can't plan or grow, because it doesn't know when the commission will arrive. Large brokerages can push developers to pay; smaller ones can't, and many of them don't survive. Every brokerage, large or small, would spend money sooner on campaigns that sell more for the developers.

Indicative timeline.

At the end of the chain is the broker. Work starts in month one and the deal closes in about two months. The commission comes two to three months after that, so the broker waits four to five months for one paycheck while the bills keep coming. A broker who wants to move to another agency or leave has to wait until the commission is paid.

With Aaronz, the brokerage is paid at the close: at the start of month 3 Aaronz pays the brokerage through the finance partner, which funds the two months.
