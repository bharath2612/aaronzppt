02The problem · Developers

Developers carry commission costs on every sale

Paying brokers on time strains cash flow.

Developerpays commission toBrokeragepays a share toBroker

The need

A partner that pays brokers now and is repaid later

Most exposed

Smaller developers

Limited access to funding, so commission is paid from project cash.

The commission starts with the developer. Every sale creates a commission bill, and paying every broker on time strains cash flow. Developers would like a partner who pays the brokers now and lets the developer pay later. Smaller developers have it worst, because they can't raise funds the way large developers can.

Earlier payment would fund the marketing that drives developer sales.

Next in line is the brokerage. It can't plan or grow, because it doesn't know when the commission will arrive. Large brokerages can push developers to pay; smaller ones can't, and many of them don't survive. Every brokerage, large or small, would spend money sooner on campaigns that sell more for the developers.

At the end of the chain is the broker. Work starts in month one and the deal closes in about two months. The commission comes two to three months after that, so the broker waits four to five months for one paycheck while the bills keep coming. A broker who wants to move to another agency or leave has to wait until the commission is paid.

Aaronz pays the brokerage through a finance partner, and the developer repays the partner.

This is where Aaronz fits. Today a deal takes about two months to close, and then the brokerage waits about two more months for the developer to pay. With Aaronz, the brokerage is paid at the close: at the start of month 3 Aaronz pays the brokerage through the finance partner, which funds the two months. At the end of month 4 the developer pays the money back to the finance partner on its usual dates, and Aaronz then takes its fee from the funder. The developer's timeline doesn't change; the brokerage and broker no longer wait.
