Ready homes use the same product as off-plan, secured by the buyer’s commission cheque. When the MOU is signed, the buyer gives the usual 10% deposit as two cheques: 8% as the deposit, and 2% for the broker’s commission, made out to the brokerage. The brokerage chooses its payment terms and raises the invoice. AaronzPay holds the 2% cheque and approves, and the finance partner pays the commission amount to the brokerage. On transfer day the brokerage cashes the 2% cheque and repays the finance partner, which settles and pays AaronzPay its fee. The commission cheque cannot be taken back, so it is still cashed if the deal falls through. This works for cash, mortgage and mixed deals. Transfer is about 2 to 4 weeks after the MOU for cash deals and about 6 to 8 weeks for mortgage deals.

Paying a brokerage at registration instead of about 50 days later has a cost, and someone has to pay it. Take a AED 100,000 commission and a 1.5% charge. In Option 1 the brokerage pays: it receives AED 98,500 at registration, and the developer pays the usual AED 100,000 on its usual date, so nothing changes for the developer. In Option 2 the brokerage and the developer share the charge: the brokerage receives AED 99,250 and the developer pays AED 100,750. Either way the finance partner earns the same AED 1,500 and pays AaronzPay its share. These are example figures; the rate, the split and AaronzPay's share are still to be agreed.

Credit risk

08Credit risk

Short, confirmed and checked before every payment

The developerowes the money
About 50 daysfrom payout to repayment
Confirmedby the developer at registration

  1. 01Before paying · Day 0

    • Brokerage, broker card, project and marketing agreement checked
    • Paid only once, on a confirmed sale

  2. 02While waiting · Day 0 to 50

    • Overdue past a set number of days: no new payouts on that developer's sales
    • Each developer stays under its limit

  3. 03If it goes wrong · After day 50

    • Sale cancelled: the developer still repays
    • Late payment: the developer pays interest for each day overdue
The finance partner's risk is simple to describe: the developer owes the money, for about 50 days, on a commission the developer has already confirmed at registration. Protection works in three stages. Before paying, AaronzPay checks that the brokerage, broker, project and marketing agreement are registered, and pays only on a confirmed registration and only once per unit. While waiting, if a developer is overdue past a set number of days, AaronzPay stops new payouts on that developer's sales, and exposure to each developer stays under its limit. If something goes wrong: a late developer pays interest for each day overdue, which is already agreed; the rule for a cancelled sale is still to be decided.
