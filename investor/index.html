The gap

Repaid by the brokerage

We found no UAE player publishing the LytePay setup: a live commission balance, repaid by the brokerage.

Illustrative. 4.5% flat fee and 80% limit are LytePay's published Singapore terms. 9% cost of money, 60 days and 5% take-up are assumptions. $3.7B commissions: DLD; 50% agent share: Bayut. Before VAT, operating costs and credit losses.

LytePay in Singapore is not a loan: agents pay a one-time 4.5% fee, get paid within a business day, and the agency repays LytePay from commissions as they arrive. Any shortfall can be recovered from the agent, and the old agency keeps repaying if the agent moves. In the UAE, $3.7B of commission split 50:50 means about $1.85B a year goes to around 40,000 brokers. If 5% of that cashes out at 80%, we advance $74M a year. A flat fee also protects us on duration: at a 9% cost of money, a 4.5% fee only stops making money after 180 days. Regulation is the open question, because this is money to individuals.

08Who we partner with

Every developer tested before we sign

We start with reputed developers that hold a large share of the market. Our internal tools then test each one on five things.

Will we get paid?

Reputation

Well regarded by brokers and buyers.

Cash flow

Strong finances and manageable debt.

Payment record

A proven history of paying dues on time.

Is the volume there?

Market share

A large share of sales in the market.

Unsold inventory

Stock still to sell, so the volume keeps coming.

If they don't pass all five, we don't sign.

Aaronz pays the brokerage first and collects from the developer later. The first three tests protect our money: will they stand behind it, can they pay, and do they actually pay? Reputation covers market and social reputation. Cash flow covers balance sheets, leverage and banking history. The last two tests are about the size of the opportunity: market share gives us volume today, and unsold inventory shows the volume still to come. We start with reputed developers with a large market share, then widen as we build a repayment history.
